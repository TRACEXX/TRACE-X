ANALYST NOTE - HOW THE ETHEREUM HOT WALLET WAS EMPTIED (EIP-7702)
Case: Binance hot-wallet theft, 29 October 2026
Prepared for TRACE X training from public reporting (main source: TRM Labs, on-chain data as of 30 Oct 2026 00:30 UTC)
Status: Binance has NOT published an official root cause. Points marked [ASSESSMENT] are analysts' opinions, not confirmed facts.

1. BACKGROUND: WHAT IS A HOT WALLET?
   An exchange keeps most coins offline ("cold") and a working balance online ("hot") so that customer
   withdrawals can be paid quickly. A hot wallet is controlled by a private key held in the exchange's
   signing system. Anyone who can make that system SIGN a message can move the hot wallet's funds.

2. BACKGROUND: WHAT IS EIP-7702?
   EIP-7702 arrived with Ethereum's "Pectra" upgrade in May 2025. It lets a normal wallet sign an
   "authorization" that says: "from now on, run the code of smart contract X when someone calls me".
   It was designed for useful features (batching, sponsored fees). But if an attacker obtains such an
   authorization pointing to THEIR contract, the wallet starts obeying the attacker's code.

3. WHAT THE ATTACKER DID (sequence reconstructed by TRM Labs)
   a) Prepared a "sweeper" smart contract that only the attacker's operator wallet can call.
      Calling it sends everything in the wallet to the attacker.
   b) Obtained 50 authorizations signed by the Binance hot wallet, each pointing to the sweeper contract.
   c) Why 50? Each authorization is only valid for one specific "nonce" (the wallet's transaction counter).
      The hot wallet's nonce keeps rising as normal customer withdrawals go out, so the attacker could not
      know the exact number in advance. Fifty signatures covering a range of nonces guaranteed that one
      would match at the moment of the attack.
   d) 20:20 UTC: the operator submitted ONE transaction containing all 50 authorizations. The matching one
      activated the delegation.
   e) About 20:23:24 UTC (3 min 24 s later): the full balance - 167,464 ETH and 63 tokens including
      1.615 billion USDT - was swept to the attacker's main receiving wallet.
   f) The delegation stayed in place, so later deposits into the hot wallet were swept again at
      20:41, 20:59, 21:04, 21:44, 22:39 and 23:56 UTC.
   g) The same wallet was used to operate a separate Binance smart account and pull 823 million USDC.

4. OTHER BLOCKCHAINS
   Bitcoin, Tron, XRP Ledger, Solana, Avalanche, Polygon, Arbitrum, Optimism and Base do not use EIP-7702.
   Funds there left through ordinary signed transfers. That means the attacker could make Binance's
   systems sign transactions on many chains.
   [ASSESSMENT - TRM Labs] This points to a compromise of Binance's hot-wallet SIGNING INFRASTRUCTURE,
   not phishing alone.

5. HOW DID THE ATTACKER GET SIGNING ACCESS? (NOT CONFIRMED)
   - Binance's initial investigation (as reported) traced the breach to a software engineer's laptop that
     gave the attacker a route to submit unauthorised withdrawals.
   - Later reporting (The New York Times, as cited on Wikipedia) says other issues were found as well.
   - No official post-mortem has been published. Record this as an OPEN QUESTION, not a finding.

6. PREPARATION (shows planning; at least 36 hours according to reports)
   - 28 Oct, morning: operator wallet funded with three small withdrawals from Tornado Cash (a mixer that
     hides where money came from).
   - Early 29 Oct: first attack code deployed and tested on two wallets.
   - Later 29 Oct: final version rehearsed on a third wallet; test funds sent back to the operator.
   - About 4 hours later: the same code was used against the Binance hot wallet.

7. WARNING SIGNS AN EXCHANGE CAN WATCH FOR (TRM Labs recommendations)
   - Any EIP-7702 delegation appearing on a treasury wallet -> alert immediately.
   - Requests to sign many authorizations across a range of nonces -> treat as a compromise indicator.
   - Keep a tested, pre-approved way to sign a revocation.
   - Separate signing systems and approval paths by chain and by wallet tier.

8. WHAT THIS NOTE DOES NOT SHOW
   It does not identify any individual, and it does not prove who obtained the signatures. Attribution to a
   group (see suspect record) comes from blockchain-intelligence firms, not from this note.
